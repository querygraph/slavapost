### Why CDIF4EOSC should matter for all of you

Research today increasingly depends on the ability to discover, integrate, and reuse data across disciplines, infrastructures, and national boundaries. Yet interoperability remains one of the greatest challenges facing the European research ecosystem, and a knowledge graph built on human-curated ground truth should provide researchers with the opportunity to rely on sovereign AI while ensuring a complete provenance chain for their data.

CDIF4EOSC addresses this challenge by building upon the Cross-Domain Interoperability Framework (CDIF) and transforming it into a practical implementation framework for EOSC, research infrastructures, and emerging European Data Spaces. We will move data stewardship beyond traditional metadata management toward a modern data engineering approach, where data, variables, provenance, quality, and governance become machine-actionable and reusable at scale. With Dataverse repository as a key data platform, we'll continue development of AI-Ready data landscape.

Although initiated in Europe, the project aims to deliver infrastructure components and interoperability capabilities worldwide, supporting collaborations across Africa, the Americas, and beyond.

At the same time, the project recognizes a major opportunity presented by Agentic AI and human-in-the-loop systems. Rather than replacing human expertise, we're going to work on the creation of library for humans and AI, and accelerate metadata creation, improve data quality, and support FAIR implementation across diverse research domains.

Without any doubt, at the heart of the project is the CDIF4EOSC Playbook - a practical, step-by-step implementation guide designed to help organizations adopt CDIF profiles and FAIR practices. The project will evolve through continuous collaboration with researchers, infrastructures, EOSC nodes, and data spaces. Our goal is not merely to document standards but to make interoperability implementable in real-world environments.

There is also a great opportunity for the Semantic Croissant, an "agent-ready" extension of the ML Croissant metadata standard designed to guide and navigate AI through structured expert knowledge. It serves as a "GPS" for data, allowing autonomous agents to locate, understand, and route queries across distributed repositories with machine-level precision.

Semantic Croissant has different layers serving own purposes:

- ML Croissant Foundation: The base layer is a standardized, machine-readable metadata vocabulary that provides the structural information (format, nature, and provenance) required to load datasets into machine learning tools.
- Cross-Domain Interoperability Framework (CDIF): This acts as the "lingua franca" for the system, facilitating a transparent transition of data between different domains by providing a set of implementation recommendations for discovery and integration. CDIF will provide AI-Ready variable descriptions with rich semantic description of the "core feature" of a dataset - the observable property that was actually measured. CDIF also uses a "variable cascade" to define complex indicators including precise units of measurement and class hierarchies expressed as a knowledge graph.
- Decentralized Identifiers (DID): These serve as the "Trust" layer in the knowledge graph. Globally unique and verifiable, DIDs can be used to assign verifiable credentials to humans and AI agents. We're also planning to wrap every LLM prompt and response with DID, preserving a complete trace of provenance and proving ownership of intellectual property.
- Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL): This standard defines digital policies and fine-grained access permissions. It ensures that autonomous agents can only access privileged or proprietary data when they have explicitly verified authorization. Supported by Machine-Actionable Provenance (PROV-O), it adopts the W3C PROV-O model to record a "chain-of-custody," documenting how data was derived and identifying the agents (human or software) responsible for every processing step.

We think it's going to be a game changer for responsible AI and can provide and enforce a lot of other features in other projects:

- Restoring Verifiability: As AI moves toward "semantics-in-models" (where LLMs guess meaning), Semantic Croissant restores verifiability by providing a formal, machine-checked layer of truth.
- Enabling Agentic AI: It is specifically built for the "agentic" era, providing the metadata necessary for autonomous systems to discover and use data while respecting privacy, licensing, and complex scientific constraints.
- Scientific Precision: It resolves ambiguity in cross-disciplinary research by harmonizing data. For instance, it uses AI and human-in-the-loop verification to ensure units of measurement (like Celsius vs. Kelvin) are explicitly defined and transformed correctly before integration.
- Governance by Design: By embedding ODRL and Data Use Ontology (DUO) tags directly into the metadata, it turns datasets into self-describing objects that can actively enforce their own usage policies within automated workflows.

Alongside the CDIF Playbook and AI assistance, we're going to deliver a lot of useful products:

- New CDIF profiles
- AI-assisted tools and services
- Integration guides for EOSC nodes
- Governance and sustainability recommendations
- Training resources and capacity-building materials
- Demonstrators based on real-world use cases

### Work Packages Driving Innovation in Europe

The project is structured around a series of interconnected work packages that collectively address standards, quality, tooling, implementation, adoption, and governance. We don't want to build everything in isolation, the idea is to establish efficient collaboration with other projects and contribute to their own digital infrastructure for the start.

### WP1 – CDIF4EOSC Playbook

Led by CODATA, WP1 will develop the roadmap, implementation guidance, and sustainability strategy for CDIF4EOSC. Building upon the recently released CDIF 1.1 baseline, the work package will guide the evolution of an enhanced CDIF framework tailored to EOSC requirements.

### WP2 – Data Quality

Trustworthy reuse requires trustworthy data. WP2 is lead by [Thomas Exner](https://www.linkedin.com/in/thomasexner/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) and focuses on integrating data quality directly into the CDIF framework by documenting and operationalizing quality dimensions such as accuracy, completeness, and consistency. The objective is to enable machine-actionable quality assessment and move toward a model of "trust through documentation."

### WP3 – AI-Augmented Tools and Services

WP3 will be managed by 
[Darren Bell](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/darren-bell-40402936a?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  from 
 [UK Data Service](https://uk.linkedin.com/company/uk-data-service?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  and will develop AI-assisted tools, APIs, and services that simplify the creation of CDIF-compliant metadata and FAIR Digital Objects. Development will begin with prototyping and requirements gathering before moving into production-ready implementations supported by modern CI/CD practices and sustainable software engineering approaches.

### WP4 – EOSC Nodes and Data Space Integration

WP4 focuses on interoperability between EOSC nodes and European Data Spaces and led by 
 [EGI Foundation](https://nl.linkedin.com/company/egi-foundation?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
 . The work includes mapping interoperability gaps, developing semantic interoperability layers, and demonstrating integration pathways through environments such as the Green Deal Data Space and the Common European Agricultural Data Space.

Technologies such as I-ADOPT, 
[FAIR-IMPACT EU Project](https://it.linkedin.com/in/fair-impact-eu-project-012764237?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  methodologies, and semantic harmonization frameworks will play a central role here.

### WP5 – Real-World Use Cases

The success of CDIF4EOSC will ultimately be measured through implementation and managed by 
[Alexandra Kokkinaki](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/alexandra-kokkinaki-3bb4935?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  from NOC-BODC. Three flagship use cases will validate project outcomes:

Ocean Sciences Using AI-assisted metadata tools to streamline the flow of information from scientists to repositories, aggregators, and EOSC services.

Social Sciences and Environment Creating data integration hubs that connect diverse datasets through geospatial, temporal, and conceptual identifiers, reducing manual data handling and improving cross-domain research.

Safe and Sustainable-by-Design Materials Connecting heterogeneous data sources and enabling semantic interoperability through shared ontologies and harmonization services.

These use cases will provide continuous feedback to ensure that the Playbook, tools, and standards remain grounded in operational reality. If it sounds relevant for you - don't hesitate to approach us and ask questions.

### WP6 – Integration into EOSC Structures

WP6 co-led by 
 [CNRS](https://fr.linkedin.com/company/cnrs?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
 /
 [EGI Foundation](https://nl.linkedin.com/company/egi-foundation?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  and will define concrete pathways for integrating CDIF4EOSC outputs into EOSC governance and operational structures. Activities include developing technical integration guides, engaging EOSC stakeholders, performing cost-benefit analyses, and producing governance recommendations that support long-term sustainability and adoption.

### Communication, Training, and Community Building

Work Package 7, led by 
 [G.A.C. Group (GAC) France](https://fr.linkedin.com/company/gacgroup?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  with support from 
 [GO FAIR Foundation](https://www.linkedin.com/company/go-fair-foundation?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  and CODATA, will ensure that project results reach the widest possible audience.

The consortium emphasized that communication is not the responsibility of a single work package - it is a shared responsibility across the entire project.

### Governance and Project Management

WP8 provides the coordination framework necessary to support a complex multi-partner initiative. Through structured governance mechanisms - including the General Assembly, Project Executive Board, External Advisory Board, and Stakeholder Forum - the project aims to ensure quality, transparency, risk management, and effective collaboration throughout its lifecycle.

The consortium enters the project with strong momentum, a clear vision, and a shared commitment to making FAIR implementation more practical, scalable, and impactful.

As 
[Simon Hodson](https://fr.linkedin.com/in/simon-hodson-b3711a11?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)
  said during the opening session: "There is a moment for this work - and that moment is now."

With the convergence of FAIR principles, semantic interoperability, data engineering, and AI-assisted stewardship, CDIF4EOSC is already positioned to become an important contributor to the future evolution of EOSC and the broader European research ecosystem.
